# Product discovery

Use this when the interviewer is deciding what to learn, not while you are coaching a live line.

## Start from a decision

Name the product decision the study should change. A study that only collects opinions about a feature idea is not ready. Write the assumption in this form: who, in what situation, does what today, with what pain, how often, and what they do instead.

Separate three layers in every note:

- Observation: what they did or said, tied to a quote.
- Inference: what you think that means.
- Recommendation: what the team should change. Hold this until more than one participant supports it, unless one story reveals a hard constraint.

## Who to recruit

Recruit people who did the job in the last few weeks. Skip proxies who only have an opinion about those people.

For the clinic app, start with staff who search for an owner and record a visit. Talk to veterinarians when the question is about reading the record later. Talk to pet owners only when the question is about their experience outside this app. They are not users of the app today.

Five to eight focused sessions beat a long unfocused one. Stop when new sessions repeat the same stories and the remaining questions are about size, not existence.

## What counts as evidence

Prefer a specific last time ("Last Tuesday I…") over a hypothetical ("I would…"). Ask for frequency, time, stakes, and the workaround. A feature request is a lead. The story behind it is the evidence.

Do not treat a gap in the software as a user need. Confirm it in an interview first.

## Planning reply

End a planning turn with:

- Decision the study should change
- Assumption under test
- Who to recruit, and who to skip
- What would change your mind
- The interview guide, saved with `save_discovery` kind `interview_guide`
